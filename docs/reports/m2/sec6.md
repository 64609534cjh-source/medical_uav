The set network is the one in our proposal, with two adjustments: only the PICK actions are scored by the shared scorer, because DELIVER, CHARGE, RETURN and WAIT do not belong to one order; and orders and cargo have separate encoders, because their features differ. The MLP and imitation learning are additions. The reward drops the proposal's waiting, first-overdue, end-of-day and empty-travel penalties for delivery, expiry and energy terms, which in the planner test above balanced timely service and late rescues better; the on-time rate stays our main metric. The environment's own changes (order expiry; travel-and-charge actions to 60, 80 or 100 % instead of MOVE and interval charging) are explained in the M1 report. Only the first 12 waiting orders are represented individually, not all requests as proposed; the rest of the backlog is summarised by counts. We still use one map rather than the proposed randomised maps, to compare learning cleanly with the baselines first; the travel times in the observation prepare a later test on unseen maps.

Our main lesson is that per-decision accuracy is a weak guide: a network that copies 94% of the teacher's decisions still serves fewer orders on time, so we judge every policy by whole days on held-out demand. The visualization feedback taught us that an animation must be paced for a reader, not for the data. Next we train both networks with our own masked PPO, from scratch and from the imitation weights, and compare them with all baselines and a rollout planner.
