**Changes from the proposal.** The set network keeps the proposal's design with two changes: the shared scorer handles only PICK actions, because DELIVER, CHARGE, RETURN and WAIT do not belong to one order; and orders and cargo get separate encoders, because their features differ. The MLP control and imitation learning were added to test the design and the training pipeline before reinforcement learning. M1 had already replaced the proposal's waiting, overdue, end-of-day and empty-travel penalties with delivery, expiry and energy terms; M2 revises the late-delivery and expiry terms (Section 3).

**Simplifications.** Only 12 waiting orders get detailed slots, with those that can still be on time first; the others count towards the backlog but cannot be picked up until they enter a slot. We use one fixed map instead of the proposed random maps, and one training seed per network. Whether the networks carry over to new maps, and whether the set network beats the MLP, are tested in the next milestone.

**Lessons.** High agreement with the teacher did not guarantee equal performance over a day, so we judge policies on complete unseen days rather than on per-decision accuracy alone. The reward comparison showed a trade-off rather than a free gain: more orders on time, slightly fewer served; we now check reward changes against both measures. And the M1 feedback taught us to pace a visualization for the reader, not for the data.

**Next.** We will train both networks with our own masked PPO, from scratch and from the imitation weights, with five training seeds each, and compare them with each other, with all baselines and with stronger planners on test days never used before. We will also train a version on many random maps, as proposed, and test it on maps it has never seen.
