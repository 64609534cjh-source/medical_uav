Before reinforcement learning, we checked that both networks can learn sensible decisions by imitating Slack-aware, our strongest rule (behaviour cloning; `bc.py`, `scripts/train_bc.py`). Slack-aware first unloads at its current stop and collects orders there for destinations already on board; otherwise it decides whether to charge and follows the earliest deadline, leaving orders that would be late anyway for last. We recorded 88,744 of its decisions on 900 days (demand seeds 0–299 at each load) and split the seeds 80/20 into training and validation. Training minimises the masked cross-entropy to the teacher's action (Adam, learning rate 3·10⁻⁴, batch 256, 30 epochs) and keeps the epoch with the lowest validation loss; the value head stays untrained until PPO.

The networks agree with the teacher on 93.5% (MLP) and 93.9% (set network) of validation decisions, yet at medium and high load their mean daily on-time rates are 1.5–3.2 percentage points below Slack-aware's (Table 1; for the set network both paired 95% intervals include zero). Small mistakes likely lead to situations the demonstrations never showed, where more mistakes follow; PPO, the next step, learns from the network's own actions and rewards instead.
