We revised the reward to put on-time delivery first. In M1 the reward for a late delivery fell from nearly +1 towards −0.5 at expiry, and an expired order cost 0.5. In an earlier prototype study at medium load, a rollout planner that sees only visible orders reached 83.1% on time and 96.6% served with the M1 reward. With the new reward (+1 on time, +0.1 late, −0.1 expired, −0.001 per battery point) it reached 89.7% on time and 94.6% served: better punctuality for a small loss in completion. We added the 7 × 7 matrix of travel times between all nodes (49 features) so the network can compare trips between stops directly. We also added FCFS (oldest request first) and nearest-first (the pickup or delivery that finishes soonest) as baselines. `scripts/evaluate.py` runs policies and networks on identical days and reports paired bootstrap intervals; separate demand days are reserved for development, model selection and final testing.
